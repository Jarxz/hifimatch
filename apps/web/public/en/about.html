About The Hifi Match

A hi-fi compatibility engine: it says whether the components of a system work together and what they deliver in a real room, with every rule out in the open.

What it is

The Hifi Match is a hi-fi compatibility engine: it checks whether the components of an audio system work together and what they actually deliver in a real room. It runs entirely in the browser, with no accounts and no payments.

Two layers, always declared

Every result is split into two layers, always declared. The physics layer has a formula, a threshold, a data source, and a confidence level — it's falsifiable, and that's what makes it defensible: a threshold can be argued with evidence. The editorial-criteria layer is preferences the site declares as such (for example, how several severities get grouped into one verdict), and it's never mixed visually with the physics.

What it evaluates

The engine evaluates power margin against the room's peaks, the load and impedance the amplifier sees, impedance bridging and volume headroom when a streamer or DAC is added, the room's axial modes, and the estimated reverberation time (RT60). A missing spec is never shown as passing: it's marked "no data," and the reason is stated.

What it never says

The engine never issues judgments about tonal character, predictions of sonic synergy between brands, or quality comparisons between products — that isn't calculated, and there's no plan to make it. The final listening call stays with each person; the site provides the data and the physical mechanism behind each one.

Limits, declared

Manufacturers publish output impedance, impedance curves and, sometimes, real sensitivity (measured in half-space and overstated) with uneven quality. That's why every spec carries its own source and confidence level, and a rule that runs on low-confidence data degrades its own confidence and states so.

Room rules start from a rigid rectangular box, an approximation that's easy to get wrong: none of them issues an error-level verdict — the ceiling is a warning, always with the caveat that it's verified by measuring, not calculating. Modes only consider axial ones, and reverberation time is given as an estimated range, not a verdict, because the site doesn't measure the room.

Equipment found through online search isn't part of the curated catalog: it enters with low confidence and its source visible, and data outside a reasonable physical range is discarded. The overall verdict (Power, Electrical coupling, Room) is the site's editorial criterion: it takes the worst component of each group, and it's labeled as such on screen.

Who it's for

It's built for anyone assembling or reviewing an audio system who wants a physics-based check before making a purchase.
